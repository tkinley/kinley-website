Likelihood and maximum likelihood

A likelihood measures how the observed data support different parameter values under a model. Once the data are fixed, you vary the parameter and compare likelihoods. The maximum likelihood estimate is the parameter value that makes the observed data most likely.

Intuition

The same eight successes look more plausible under a high success rate than under a low one. For independent observations, logs turn products of probabilities into a sum.

Worked math

  1. For 8 successes in 10 Bernoulli trials, the likelihood is proportional to p⁸(1 − p)².
  2. The log-likelihood is 8 log(p) + 2 log(1 − p) plus a constant. Its derivative is 8/p − 2/(1 − p).
  3. Setting this to zero gives 8(1 − p) = 2p, so p̂ = 0.8. The second derivative is negative at the solution.

In bioinformatics

Likelihood-based models fit assay pass rates, abundance counts, and outcome probabilities by choosing an appropriate distribution and mean structure.

Bias, variance, and shrinkage

Bias is the difference between an estimator’s expected value and the target. Variance describes how much the estimate fluctuates across samples. Mean squared error decomposes into variance plus squared bias. Shrinkage can improve accuracy by accepting some bias in exchange for reduced variance.

Intuition

A noisy estimate rests on sparse evidence. Pulling it toward a shared baseline limits how far random noise can send it. Strong evidence should generally move the estimate less toward the baseline.

Worked math

  1. An unbiased estimate with variance 0.09 has MSE 0.09.
  2. A shrunken estimate with bias −0.10 and variance 0.04 has MSE 0.04 + 0.10² = 0.05.
  3. The advantage depends on how far the target is from the shrinkage center and how much noise is removed.

In bioinformatics

Across thousands of features, shrinkage can stabilize effect sizes and variance estimates. Report the method and the scale used.

Confidence intervals and coverage

A confidence interval procedure is designed to cover a fixed parameter at a stated long-run rate under its assumptions. The estimate and interval endpoints vary across samples. Its width reflects sampling uncertainty on the effect scale.

Intuition

Imagine drawing many studies and marking their intervals on the same ruler. The target sits in one place, and the intervals move around it.

Worked math

  1. Under an applicable normal approximation, estimate ± 1.96 × SE gives an approximate 95% interval.
  2. For a difference estimate of 0.08 with SE 0.056, the interval is 0.08 ± 0.1098, or about [−0.03, 0.19].
  3. On the percentage-point scale, this runs from about −3 to +19. Interpretation depends on the target and the assumptions used to compute the SE.

In bioinformatics

Report intervals with effect measures. An inconclusive directional comparison can still rule out effects outside the interval under the model.

Intervals with sparse counts

The Wald interval plugs an observed rate into its own standard error. At rates of zero or one, this formula collapses to zero width. Wilson’s score interval uses the hypothesized probability inside the variance and retains uncertainty near a boundary.

Intuition

Observing zero failures in ten runs is plausible even if the true failure rate is 10%. The sample allows a range of plausible rates.

Worked math

  1. For 0/10, the Wald standard error is √(0 × 1/10) = 0. It produces [0, 0].
  2. At zero successes, the Wilson 95% upper limit is z²/(n + z²), with z = 1.96.
  3. Compute 3.8416/13.8416 ≈ 0.278. The lower limit is zero.

In bioinformatics

Small assay studies and low counts need interval methods suited to sparse data, the sampling design, and the quantity being estimated.

Bootstrap uncertainty and resampling units

The nonparametric bootstrap approximates the sampling distribution by repeatedly sampling observed units with replacement and recalculating the statistic. Its validity depends on how representative those units are and whether the resampling scheme respects the design.

Intuition

Treat the observed sample as a stand-in for the population. Resample at the same level at which a new study would collect independently sampled units.

Worked math

  1. For 12 independent specimens, a bootstrap draw samples 12 specimen IDs with replacement.
  2. Recompute the mean difference or model statistic on each draw.
  3. Use the SD of the resampled estimates as a bootstrap SE. If specimens contain technical repeats, carry their repeats along when a specimen is drawn.

In bioinformatics

For multi-sample assays, a specimen bootstrap and a record bootstrap answer different questions about variability.
