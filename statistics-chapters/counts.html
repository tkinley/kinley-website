Exposure and offsets

Exposure measures the opportunity to accumulate counts. An offset is a known term added to a model’s linear predictor with coefficient fixed at 1. With a log link, the log exposure offset scales the expected count while the fitted coefficients describe rates.

Intuition

The more deeply you sample, the more events you can count. You want the model to expect higher counts from extra opportunity before attributing any difference to biology.

Worked math

  1. Write E[Yⱼ] = sⱼ × rⱼ. Taking logs gives log E[Yⱼ] = log(sⱼ) + log(rⱼ).
  2. In Figure 6, 100/10 = 10 and 200/20 = 10 counts per million reads.
  3. Doubling s adds log(2) to the log mean while the rate stays fixed.

In bioinformatics

Use offsets or model-specific size factors when fitting count data. Total library size alone can be inadequate if sample composition shifts.

The negative binomial and overdispersion

A Poisson count has equal mean and variance. Biological count data can remain more variable than that after accounting for exposure and available covariates. A common negative binomial parameterization models variance as μ + αμ², where α is dispersion.

Intuition

Specimens can have different underlying rates even within a condition. Mixing those rates adds variability beyond the randomness of counting events at a fixed rate.

Worked math

  1. At mean μ = 20, the Poisson variance is 20.
  2. With α = 0.1, the negative binomial variance is 20 + 0.1 × 20² = 60.
  3. The SD increases from √20 ≈ 4.47 to √60 ≈ 7.75. Underestimating variance can make interval estimates too narrow.

In bioinformatics

Inspect the mean–variance relationship across features and use biological replicates to learn about dispersion.

Relative abundance and size factors

Sequencing measures composition under finite sampling depth. A very abundant feature can consume a large share of reads and change the observed shares of other features. A size-factor method uses assumptions about the bulk of features to estimate sample-to-sample scaling.

Intuition

Imagine filling the same number of slots from two mixtures. Adding more of one component can reduce the representation of the rest even if their absolute amounts stayed constant.

Worked math

  1. For A = (10,20,400) and B = (20,40,400), geometric reference counts are about (14.14,28.28,400).
  2. Ratios for A are (0.707,0.707,1), and for B they are (1.414,1.414,1). Their median factors are 0.707 and 1.414.
  3. The first two features then align on the scaled count measure. This calculation depends on the choice of reference and is not a measurement of absolute concentration.

In bioinformatics

Check normalization assumptions when many features shift together. External controls or another justified reference may be needed for absolute claims.

Borrowing information across features

Estimating a separate dispersion for every feature from a few replicates is noisy. Empirical Bayes and related methods estimate a shared pattern across features, then moderate individual estimates toward that pattern according to their information.

Intuition

A feature measured only a few times cannot reliably tell you its own variability. Features with similar abundance provide a useful baseline when that sharing assumption fits.

Worked math

  1. As a simplified illustration, combine a noisy estimate 0.40 with a trend value 0.10 using weights 0.25 and 0.75.
  2. The moderated value is 0.25 × 0.40 + 0.75 × 0.10 = 0.175.
  3. Actual methods may moderate on a transformed scale and choose strength from the data. This arithmetic illustrates the direction, not a software formula.

In bioinformatics

Distinguish moderation of variance or dispersion from shrinkage of effect sizes. They address different quantities.

Transformation for plots and models

Transformations make visualization and modeling easier when variability changes with the mean. A log can compress large values and turn ratios into differences. The scale and assumptions that make a plot readable are not necessarily the ones a count likelihood needs.

Intuition

A twofold change means the same relative change at 10 and 1,000 counts. A log scale shows equal fold changes as equal distances.

Worked math

  1. log₂(20) − log₂(10) = log₂(20/10) = 1. The same applies to 200/100.
  2. At zero, log is undefined. Adding a constant changes low-count ratios substantially: log₂(2) − log₂(1) = 1 for a 0-to-1 change after adding 1.
  3. A count GLM uses the count and an exposure offset directly. A variance-stabilized matrix can be appropriate for exploratory PCA.

In bioinformatics

Record which matrix was used for visualization, differential analysis, or prediction. The appropriate preprocessing can differ by task.

1. Compare rates at unequal depths

A feature has 100 reads in a 10-million-read library and 200 reads in a 20-million-read library. Compare the rates and explain how a count model accounts for exposure.

Show the answer

Both rates are 10 per million. In a log-link count model, log exposure is an offset with coefficient fixed at 1.
