Rows, samples, and independent units

A data table records measurements. The experimental unit is the smallest unit independently assigned to a condition, and the sampling unit is what was independently sampled. A biological claim must be supported at the appropriate level. Repeated measurements from a single unit provide information about that unit and may share its underlying variation.

Intuition

A barcode identifies a record. It tells you little about what could have varied independently. Measurements sharing a donor inherit that donor’s baseline and often resemble one another.

Worked math

  1. Six donors each contribute 100 cells, giving 600 cell records. If the condition is assigned to donors, there are six independent treatment assignments.
  2. A donor-level analysis can summarize cells within donors. A multilevel model can retain individual cells and account for variation between donors.

In bioinformatics

For single-cell condition comparisons, keep donor identifiers and choose an analysis whose uncertainty reflects the donor-level design.

Biological and technical replication

Biological replicates provide information about variation across independent biological units. Technical replicates help estimate the variability of an assay or measurement for a given unit. A good design identifies both sources of variability.

Intuition

Repeating a mass-spectrometry run can tell you that a signal is measured consistently. Measuring a new specimen tells you whether the signal is common across specimens.

Worked math

  1. A simple model writes a measurement as yᵢⱼ = μ + bᵢ + eᵢⱼ. The specimen effect bᵢ is shared across repeats. Technical error eᵢⱼ varies by repeat.
  2. If their variances are σᵦ² and σₑ², the variance of a specimen mean from m independent technical repeats is σᵦ² + σₑ²/m.
  3. More repeats reduce σₑ²/m. The between-specimen variance remains.

In bioinformatics

Use technical replicates to assess reproducibility and biological replicates to estimate variation across specimens.

The target comparison

An estimand is the quantity the analysis aims to learn. Specify the population, outcome, comparison, and scale. The estimate is the number calculated from the observed data.

Intuition

An effect can be large on one scale and modest on another. A change from 2% to 4% doubles the rate, while the absolute change is two percentage points. The useful measure depends on the decision.

Worked math

  1. For detection rates 0.04 and 0.02, the absolute difference is 0.04 − 0.02 = 0.02, or +2 percentage points.
  2. The ratio is 0.04 ÷ 0.02 = 2. Swapping the reference reverses the sign of the difference and takes the reciprocal of the ratio.

In bioinformatics

Before comparing pipelines, state whether the target is detection rate, annotation agreement, prediction error, or another clearly defined outcome.

Confounding and batch effects

A batch effect is a systematic measurement difference associated with a run, site, instrument, or similar factor. Confounding arises when the comparison also tracks that factor and their effects cannot be distinguished without further design information or assumptions.

Intuition

If every control is measured on Monday and every treated specimen on Tuesday, time and treatment move together. Adding both names to a model cannot create the missing Monday-treated or Tuesday-control observations.

Worked math

  1. Write E[Y] = β₀ + β₁ × treated + β₂ × run2.
  2. If treated = run2 in every row, only β₁ + β₂ is identified. Increasing β₁ by c and decreasing β₂ by c leaves every fitted mean unchanged.
  3. Crossing both conditions with both runs supplies comparisons within run and breaks that exact alias.

In bioinformatics

Check the condition-by-batch table before fitting a model. Overlap matters even when the design columns are technically distinct.

Pairing, blocking, and missingness

Pairing compares measurements within the same matched unit. Blocking groups comparable units before comparing conditions. These structures can absorb nuisance variation when the analysis preserves them. Missing measurements can change which units the comparison represents.

Intuition

A before-and-after comparison subtracts a specimen’s baseline. It avoids conflating a high-starting specimen with a low-starting one.

Worked math

  1. Three before scores are 10, 4, and 8. The matching after scores are 12, 5, and 11.
  2. Compute within-pair differences: 2, 1, 3. Their mean is 2 and their sample SD is 1, so their SE is 1/√3 ≈ 0.58.
  3. If the third after value is missing, the complete-pair mean becomes (2 + 1)/2 = 1.5. Whether it generalizes depends on why that value was missing.

In bioinformatics

For matched assay comparisons, preserve specimen IDs and report how incomplete pairs were handled.

1. Find the independent unit

A study records 1,000 cells from each of 8 donors. The condition was assigned to donors. How many independent treatment assignments are there, and why?

Show the answer

There are 8 independent donor-level assignments. Measurements within each donor share donor-level biology and the condition.

2. Diagnose a confounded design

All control samples run on instrument 1 and all treated samples on instrument 2. Explain the problem and propose a better design.

Show the answer

The condition and instrument indicators are identical. Put observations from both conditions on both instruments so there are within-instrument comparisons.
