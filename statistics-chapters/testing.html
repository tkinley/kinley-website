Build an exact test

Concept 5.1

Choose the null and statistic

Consider a permutation test for a difference in pass rates across two production settings.

  1. Specify the statistic before looking at the result, such as the standardized difference.
  2. Use a null under which the permitted label assignments are exchangeable.
  3. Decide what counts as extreme. This chapter uses absolute distance from zero for centered two-sided statistics.

The p-value depends on the null, the statistic, and the tail rule.

Try it · What changes if you choose a one-sided test?

Only results in the prespecified direction count toward the tail probability.

Concept 5.2

Hold the margins fixed

In one stratum there are four observations, two A labels, and two successes.

  1. Fix the four outcome labels and choose which two observations receive A labels.
  2. Count assignments: choose 2 of 4, giving 4 × 3 ÷ 2 = 6.
  3. The number of successes in A can be 0, 1, or 2. B gets the remaining successes.

The stratum’s row totals and total successes stay fixed across the randomization.

Try it · Does the test shuffle all observations across strata?

No. A blocked shuffle stays inside each stratum and preserves the A and B totals there.

Concept 5.3

Count the possible tables

The three possible tables have different numbers of underlying label assignments.

  1. To give A no successes, pick both failures: 1 way.
  2. To give A one success, pick one of two successes and one of two failures: 2 × 2 = 4 ways.
  3. To give A two successes, pick both successes: 1 way. Divide each count by 6.

The success count follows a hypergeometric distribution under this fixed-margin null.

Try it · Would giving the three tables equal probability be valid here?

No. The middle table represents four of the six equally likely assignments.

Concept 5.4

Combine two strata

Take two independent strata with the same margins. Let S be the sum of the two A success counts.

  1. Each count has weights (1, 4, 1) out of 6.
  2. Multiply and group by the sum. For S = 2 there are 1×1 + 4×4 + 1×1 = 18 assignments.
  3. Across S = 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, the assignment counts are 1, 8, 18, 8, 1 out of 36.

Center S at its null expectation of 2 to measure distance from the null.

Try it · What is the probability that S = 3?

8/36 ≈ 0.222.

Concept 5.5

Sum the extreme outcomes

Suppose the observed value is S = 4. Use the two-sided rule |S − 2|.

  1. The observed distance from 2 is |4 − 2| = 2.
  2. The outcomes at least that far from 2 are S = 0 and S = 4.
  3. Add the probabilities: (1 + 1) ÷ 36 = 0.0556.

The exact two-sided p-value is about 0.056 even at the most extreme table.

Try it · What is the one-sided upper-tail probability at S = 4?

1/36 ≈ 0.0278.

Concept 5.6

Approximate the tail with random shuffles

When the number of assignments is too large to enumerate, sample valid shuffles and recompute the same statistic each time.

  1. Draw B = 999 random permutations and count the b = 19 results at least as extreme as observed.
  2. Use the plus-one construction: (b + 1) ÷ (B + 1).
  3. Compute 20 ÷ 1,000 = 0.020. Report the shuffle design and the number of draws.

The Monte Carlo resolution with 999 draws is 1/1,000. Exact test resolution depends on the possible tables and their probabilities.

Try it · With 999 draws and zero extreme shuffles, what do you report?

The plus-one calculation gives 1/1,000 = 0.001.
