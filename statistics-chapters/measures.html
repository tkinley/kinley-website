Measure an association

Read a 2×2 table and calculate three effects from the same counts.

Concept 1.1

Read the 2×2 table

Two classes log whether students submitted a worksheet. In A, 18 of 24 submitted. In B, 20 of 40 submitted.

  1. Identify the outcome: submission, yes or no.
  2. Fill the No cells by subtraction: 24 − 18 = 6 in A and 40 − 20 = 20 in B.
  3. Check every row: 18 + 6 = 24 and 20 + 20 = 40. Keep B as the reference.

The four cell counts are A = (18, 6) and B = (20, 20).

Try it · What is the total number of submissions?

18 + 20 = 38.

Concept 1.2

Calculate a proportion

What fraction in each group has the outcome? In cross-sectional data, this is the observed prevalence.

  1. Divide A’s Yes count by A’s total: 18 ÷ 24 = 0.75.
  2. Divide B’s Yes count by B’s total: 20 ÷ 40 = 0.50.
  3. Multiply each proportion by 100 to express it as a percentage.

A is at 75% and B is at 50%.

Try it · What is the proportion without the outcome in A?

6 ÷ 24 = 0.25, or 25%.

Concept 1.3

Subtract percentage points

An absolute difference tells you how far apart the two rates are.

  1. Set the direction A − B.
  2. Subtract the proportions: 0.75 − 0.50 = 0.25.
  3. Convert the difference to percentage points: 0.25 × 100 = 25 pp.

A’s observed prevalence is 25 percentage points higher.

Try it · What happens if B is placed first?

0.50 − 0.75 = −0.25, or −25 percentage points.

Concept 1.4

Divide prevalences

A prevalence ratio describes how many times as common the outcome is in A compared with B.

  1. Use B’s 0.50 as the denominator.
  2. Calculate 0.75 ÷ 0.50 = 1.50.
  3. Subtract 1 if asked for relative change: (1.50 − 1) × 100 = 50%.

A’s observed prevalence is 1.5 times B’s, a 50% relative increase.

Try it · What is the no-difference value for this ratio?

1, provided the reference prevalence is above zero.

Concept 1.5

Calculate and interpret odds

Odds compare Yes to No within a group. Odds ratios compare those two odds.

  1. A’s odds are 18 ÷ 6 = 3. B’s odds are 20 ÷ 20 = 1.
  2. Divide the odds: 3 ÷ 1 = 3. The cross-product gives the same answer: (18 × 20) ÷ (6 × 20) = 3.
  3. Convert odds back to a probability with odds ÷ (1 + odds). For A, 3 ÷ 4 = 0.75.

The same table gives an odds ratio of 3 and a prevalence ratio of 1.5.

Try it · If reference odds are 1 and the odds ratio is 2, what is A’s prevalence?

A’s odds are 2, so its prevalence is 2 ÷ 3 ≈ 66.7%.

Concept 1.6

Handle a zero cell

Suppose A has 4/24 submissions and B has 0/40. Some effect measures still have finite sample estimates.

  1. Subtract: 4/24 − 0/40 = 0.1667, or +16.7 pp.
  2. Compute odds: 4/20 = 0.20 in A and 0/40 = 0 in B.
  3. The raw ratio 0.20 ÷ 0 is infinite. Keep the observed counts visible and choose an inference method that handles sparse data.

State the counts and which effect measure is defined before interpreting a ratio.

Try it · What if both groups have zero submissions?

The observed difference is 0 pp. The ordinary odds ratio is undefined because both odds are zero.
