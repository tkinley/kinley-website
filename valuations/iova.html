My view: the core matters most

I see a business with a commercial melanoma franchise and a promising but uncertain pipeline. A lower share price after a disappointing readout does not, by itself, make the shares cheap. My question is what future cash generation the price already requires.

In my central case, the approved melanoma franchise contributes $4.98 per share before a $0.35 label-risk deduction. Lung cancer contributes only $0.11. That makes melanoma adoption, margins, competition, and TILVANCE-301 more consequential to my valuation than a modest change in the lung program.

My conclusion is cautious at the dated $8.48 reference. I can reach that price, but I have to use more favorable operating assumptions or a lower discount rate. This is my estimate under a chosen model, with substantial room for a different view.

All amounts are U.S. dollars. This is a dated personal valuation, not investment advice or a live price target. The $8.48 reference comes from my supplied 30 October notes; I do not present it as an independently verified closing quote.

How I value the pieces

I use a sum-of-the-parts risk-adjusted net present value, or rNPV. I discount three possible revenue-and-margin paths for the approved franchise, weight them, and add the pipeline after allowing for the chance that each program fails.

Equity = core DCF − expected label loss + pipeline rNPVs + next-generation option − corporate costs + net cash
Value per share = equity ÷ diluted shares

For a pipeline program, rNPV equals its success probability times the present value of commercial profits, less the present value of scheduled development spending. I charge that spending in full in this simplified model. I use a 10% discount rate alongside explicit clinical and regulatory probabilities, rather than adding an arbitrary extra premium for the same binary risks.

TILVANCE-301 affects two pieces together: the frontline opportunity and the existing label. At a 50% success probability, a 20% conditional withdrawal probability after failure, and a 70% core-value loss on withdrawal, the expected core haircut is 50% × 20% × 70% = 7%. These three inputs are opinions, not FDA forecasts.

Cash flows run from 2027 to 2045, with mid-year discounting to approximately the end of 2026. I assume 21% cash tax on positive profits from 2033, no terminal value, no takeover premium, and $40M of annual unallocated corporate costs.

What does $8.48 already require?

I reverse the calculation: keep the v2 pipeline and base margin assumptions, and solve for the core revenue peak that makes the model equal the reference price. The single core path starts at $575M in 2027, rises linearly to its peak in 2031, stays flat through 2035, then declines 10% annually.

About $1.50B in annual core revenueRequired to support $8.48 per diluted share under this particular reverse DCF.

A single path reaching $1.0B gives $6.35 per share with the same pipeline. This is a different construction from the weighted three-path central case. Under the adverse-safety assumptions, $8.48 instead requires about $1.73B of peak core revenue.

I can also close much of the gap with more favorable assumptions: the optimistic operating case is $8.27 at 9% and $8.85 at 8%. That is why I see the disagreement as one about growth, economics, and required return.

The implied peak is conditional on this revenue path, margins, pipeline, capital structure, and discount rate. It does not reveal what every market participant believes. I omit an infusion-count conversion because it would require extra assumptions about net price and the Amtagvi/Proleukin mix.

A distribution, with its assumptions visible

The supplied Python simulation produces a median of $5.69 and a 10th–90th percentile range of $2.17–$9.50 for 200,000 draws with independent program events. About 17% of draws exceed $8.48. Those are outputs of my selected distributions, not an objective 17% probability that the market price will rise.

With a shared latent-normal factor of 0.5 across program events, the range widens to $1.92–$9.78, with a $5.66 median and about 18% above the reference. The latent correlation input is not the same as the realized correlation of binary approval outcomes.

How the simulation is constructed

Seed 11; 200,000 draws. Core value uses a lognormal revenue multiplier (log standard deviation 0.32) scaled to preserve the expected core PV, plus a normal margin shift with 5 percentage points standard deviation. Program success is binary, with probabilities from the model. Conditional peak sales are lognormal, with log standard deviation 0.5 for frontline and 0.6 for other programs. The same frontline event drives the label-withdrawal branch. Development spending, net cash, corporate costs, and the next-generation option remain fixed. The model omits many real-world dependencies and financing paths.

The DCF also has practical limits. Revenue times margin is a proxy for cash flow; capex, working capital, restricted cash, taxes, and future financing need a fuller treatment. I assume 475M shares throughout. Stopping in 2045 suppresses residual value, while omitted funding needs can push in the other direction. These simplifications can materially change the answer.

What would change my mind?

  • Commercial execution: revenue against the roughly $120–125M quarterly H2 guidance requirement; patient throughput, referral trends, and gross margin. Growth needs to become durable cash generation.
  • The balance sheet: actual cash, restricted cash, share count, and any financing after June. I would replace the $240M and 475M estimates before relying on a fresh per-share result.
  • Lung durability and safety: duration of response, grade 5 events with attribution, denominators, follow-up, and outcomes by lymphodepletion regimen. Prospective validation matters more than a selected subgroup alone.
  • TILVANCE-301: its efficacy and safety evidence. This changes both frontline value and the confirmatory-risk deduction on the existing franchise.
  • Competition: actual uptake and evidence from alternatives. I would revise the scenario weights and erosion assumptions as those effects become observable.

My valuation is a set of commitments I can revisit: which evidence would make me change the sales path, margins, success probabilities, or share count? A new headline should move the line it actually affects.
