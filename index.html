I'm interested in the combinatorics of mobile genetic elements, how they move, mix, and reshape bacterial genomes. I'm still figuring out how to study all of this properly. Fortunately, I have Tom Platt helping me along the way. My consulting days left me obsessed with finding signal in noise. These days, I mostly nerd out over false discovery rates (FDR) and how easily p-values can fool us.

What the evidence supports

Population structure matters: replicon and plasmid profiles were largely lineage-associated. No tested replicon family or accessory gene cluster showed statistically significant LMF-associated enrichment after the reported FDR correction. Select a point to explore its meaning.

Compare within genetic backgrounds

Shared ancestry can confound pooled reservoir comparisons. The study restricted comparisons to mixed SNP clusters and used lineage-stratified association tests; plasmid variation was largely associated with lineage.

Sections 2.4 and 4.1–4.2

These are observational genomic comparisons, not experimental demonstrations of adaptation.

Agrobacterium on wild sunflowers

Kansas State University · Platt Lab

How do closely related bacterial groups vary within the same plant habitat? I am investigating genomic diversity and evolutionary relationships in wild sunflower-associated Agrobacterium.

In notation
core=⋂i=1nGiaccessory=(⋃i=1nGi)∖core\begin{gathered}\text{core}=\bigcap_{i=1}^{n} G_i\\[4pt] \text{accessory}=\Big(\bigcup_{i=1}^{n} G_i\Big)\setminus\text{core}\end{gathered}core=i=1⋂n​Gi​accessory=(i=1⋃n​Gi​)∖core​

GiG_iGi​ is the set of gene families in strain iii. The core is what every strain carries. The accessory part is what only some carry, and it is where mobile elements tend to live.

dJ(i,j)=1−∣Gi∩Gj∣∣Gi∪Gj∣d_J(i,j)=1-\frac{|G_i\cap G_j|}{|G_i\cup G_j|}dJ​(i,j)=1−∣Gi​∪Gj​∣∣Gi​∩Gj​∣​

Gene-content distance between two strains. Two strains can sit close together on the tree and still be far apart here.

View approach

The work combines genome quality assessment, comparative genomics, and phylogenetic analysis to examine relationships among plant-associated bacteria. Understanding this variation can inform broader questions about microbial diversity in shared habitats. The manuscript is in preparation.

Manuscript in preparation · First author
Annotation integrationGenome resources

Integrating Volvox genome annotations

Kansas State University · Olson Lab

How can successive genome releases be compared without mistaking annotation differences for biological change? I am developing approaches to integrate Volvox carteri gene annotations and evaluate their consistency.

In notation
B=(A∪B,  E)E={(a,b)∈A×B: a∼b}\begin{gathered}\mathcal{B}=(A\cup B,\;E)\\[2pt] E=\{(a,b)\in A\times B:\ a\sim b\}\end{gathered}B=(A∪B,E)E={(a,b)∈A×B: a∼b}​

AAA and BBB are the gene models from two genome releases. An edge a∼ba\sim ba∼b means sequence comparison supports them as the same locus.

type⁡(K)={one-to-one∣KA∣=∣KB∣=1split∣KA∣=1<∣KB∣merge∣KA∣>1=∣KB∣complex∣KA∣, ∣KB∣>1orphanmin⁡(∣KA∣,∣KB∣)=0\operatorname{type}(K)=\begin{cases}\text{one-to-one} & |K_A|=|K_B|=1\\ \text{split} & |K_A|=1<|K_B|\\ \text{merge} & |K_A|>1=|K_B|\\ \text{complex} & |K_A|,\,|K_B|>1\\ \text{orphan} & \min(|K_A|,|K_B|)=0\end{cases}type(K)=⎩⎨⎧​one-to-onesplitmergecomplexorphan​∣KA​∣=∣KB​∣=1∣KA​∣=1<∣KB​∣∣KA​∣>1=∣KB​∣∣KA​∣,∣KB​∣>1min(∣KA​∣,∣KB​∣)=0​

Each connected component KKK of B\mathcal{B}B, with KA=K∩AK_A=K\cap AKA​=K∩A, gets one label. Splits, merges, and orphans are where an annotation change can masquerade as biology.

View approach

The approach brings together sequence comparisons, annotation reconciliation, and genomic-context analysis, with conserved-gene and RNA-seq evidence used to assess candidate gene models. The goal is to support more reliable comparative research in a model of multicellularity. A first-author manuscript is in preparation.

Evaluating Vibrio markers in seafood

Comparative genomics · Vibrio vulnificus

Do published clinical-associated markers remain informative when evaluated against seafood isolates? I am investigating their transferability across sampling contexts, with food-safety surveillance as the motivation.

In notation
πm(s∣c)=Pr⁡(Xm=1∣S=s, C=c)\pi_m(s\mid c)=\Pr\big(X_m=1\mid S=s,\ C=c\big)πm​(s∣c)=Pr(Xm​=1∣S=s, C=c)

Xm=1X_m=1Xm​=1 when an isolate carries published marker mmm. SSS is its sampling source, clinical or seafood, and CCC is its clade.

Δm(c)=πm(clinical∣c)−πm(seafood∣c)\Delta_m(c)=\pi_m(\text{clinical}\mid c)-\pi_m(\text{seafood}\mid c)Δm​(c)=πm​(clinical∣c)−πm​(seafood∣c)

Compared within clades, not pooled. Pooled, a marker can look clinical just because clinical isolates come from a few lineages. Within a clade, the marker has to carry the signal itself.

View approach

The project uses curated public genome metadata, genome quality assessment, and phylogeny-aware comparisons to evaluate published marker candidates in a different sampling context. The aim is to assess the evidence needed before applying such markers to food-safety surveillance. Evaluation is ongoing.

Also developing: whether AI agents can help researchers assess if a conclusion in comparative bacterial genomics is supported by its evidence.
