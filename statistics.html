Statistics for reading drug trials.

Understand what a drug company’s results actually measure, how uncertain they are, and which assumptions support the conclusions. Each concept below opens its own explanation, with a worked example and common interpretation mistakes.

Start with the trial’s question and design, then examine the effect, uncertainty, and sources of bias. The examples apply across drug development; oncology terms are explained where they appear.

1. Start with the trial

Trial design, controls, and randomization
Why the comparison group, randomization, blinding, and trial phase change what a result can establish.
Endpoints and estimands
Define what was measured, in which patients, over what time, and how treatment changes were handled.
Data types, averages, and variability
Understand binary, continuous, and time-to-event data; distinguish standard deviation from standard error.
Response rates and binomial probability
Separate the observed proportion from the unknown probability, and inspect who enters the denominator.

2. Estimate the effect

Confidence intervals and the Wilson interval
Read uncertainty around an effect estimate, and distinguish confidence, credible, and prediction intervals.
Effect sizes: absolute and relative benefit
Compare risk differences, risk ratios, odds ratios, mean differences, and clinically meaningful effects.
Hypothesis tests, p-values, and significance
Understand the null hypothesis, Type I error, test choice, and why p < 0.05 does not establish clinical value.
Sample size, power, and detectable effects
See how effect assumptions, variability, event counts, alpha, and dropout shape a trial’s ability to detect benefit.

3. Check what can mislead

Multiple testing and false discoveries
Understand family-wise error, Bonferroni and Holm adjustments, testing hierarchies, and false discovery rate.
Subgroups, enrichment, and selection bias
Separate a biological hypothesis from a validated predictive biomarker, and understand post-selection optimism.
Analysis populations, missing data, and sensitivity
Understand intention-to-treat, evaluable populations, missingness assumptions, imputation, and tipping-point analyses.

4. Follow outcomes over time

Survival analysis, censoring, and hazard ratios
Read Kaplan–Meier curves, median survival, hazard ratios, log-rank tests, and restricted mean survival time.
Bayesian updating and future prediction
Connect prior, likelihood, posterior, credible intervals, and beta-binomial predictions without treating assumptions as facts.

5. Design and interpret the decision

Superiority, noninferiority, and equivalence
Understand why a failed superiority test does not prove similarity, and how a justified margin changes the question.
Interim analyses and adaptive designs
Understand alpha spending, early stopping, futility, sample-size re-estimation, and planned adaptations.
Safety statistics and benefit–risk
Read adverse-event denominators, exposure time, uncertainty around rare events, and benefit alongside harm.
